# PAIM Small-Business Lending Reference Case

## Status

Everything in this document is **PAIM-created fictional scenario design**. Harborlight Community Bank,
its people, policies, systems, applicants, evidence, and events do not describe a real organization.
Policy and authority artifacts are exercise materials, not legal or regulatory advice and not claims
about actual banking requirements.

The case takes inspiration from the source boundaries reconstructed in
[RWR Small-Business Lending Source Reconstruction](RWR_SMALL_BUSINESS_LENDING_RECONSTRUCTION.md),
but it does not extend Aster Vale's fictional history. Different names make the boundary visible.

## Exercise purpose

The exercise asks whether PAIM helps practitioners make and revisit bounded management decisions
about an AI-assisted activity while evidence, operating conditions, and feasible alternatives change.
It is deliberately capable of producing a finding that PAIM adds little value or imposes
disproportionate work.

No desired final Decision is specified.

## Organization and activity

Harborlight Community Bank serves local small businesses through five lending teams. Management is
considering **Harborlight Assist**, a vendor-hosted generative-AI capability used inside the bank's
credit-memorandum preparation workflow.

Harborlight Assist may:

- organize applicant-supplied documents;
- extract passages into a source-linked working view;
- identify missing or inconsistent information for an underwriter to review; and
- draft narrative portions of a credit memorandum.

It may not:

- approve or decline an application;
- assign a credit rating or lending grade;
- determine price, amount, or terms;
- communicate with an applicant;
- replace required source review; or
- make the final lending Decision.

Underwriters remain responsible for validating sources and drafting recommendations. An authorized
lending officer makes the lending Decision. The exercise concerns management authorization of the
AI-assisted activity, not individual loan decisions.

## PAIM Case and Configurations

### Case

**Case:** Whether and how Harborlight should use AI assistance in small-business credit-memorandum
preparation.

The Case persists across changes to the model, workflow, eligible applications, human review, and
alternatives. A material change to those conditions is represented as a Configuration change rather
than silently rewriting the Case.

### Configuration C0 - existing workflow baseline

- manual document organization and memorandum drafting;
- standardized document checklist;
- existing underwriter and lending-officer review;
- no generative-AI processing.

### Configuration C1 - bounded Harborlight Assist pilot

- current vendor model and retrieval release fixed for the pilot;
- standardized, English-language digital documents only;
- term-loan applications from established businesses;
- maximum requested amount of fictional-case currency 250,000;
- two trained senior-underwriter teams;
- source-linked draft view available throughout review;
- underwriter verifies every material statement against source documents;
- 100% quality-assurance sampling during the pilot;
- no applicant communication or automated lending action;
- eight-week duration or earlier reconsideration after a defined Trigger.

### Configuration identity rule

Changing eligible applicants, document types, model/retrieval release, material feature use, human
review, quality-assurance coverage, or operating scale may create a new Configuration or require an
explicit continuity determination. The exercise must not assume that evidence for C1 applies to a
changed Configuration.

## Fictional roles and authority materials

| Exercise role | Responsibility | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Activity sponsor | Owns and challenges the Value case. | Cannot determine the Risk conclusion or authorize use merely by sponsorship. |
| Independent risk reviewer | Develops and challenges the Risk case. | Does not own business Value judgment. |
| Evidence steward | Records source provenance and proposed Applicability. | Does not create substantive authority. |
| Configuration owner | Keeps the exact activity Configuration current. | Does not automatically have Decision Authority. |
| Decision Authority | May authorize, narrow, decline, suspend, or require further work for the exercise action. | Authority is limited to the fictional charter and exact scope. |
| Underwriter | Reviews sources and prepares an individual credit recommendation. | Retains no management authorization merely through software access. |
| Lending officer | Makes the individual loan Decision. | This is distinct from management authorization of Harborlight Assist. |

Fictional exercise policy `HCB-AI-01` prohibits autonomous lending decisions and requires source-linked
human review for the bounded pilot. Fictional charter `HCB-DA-03` assigns a named management forum
authority over the bounded pilot. These artifacts exist solely to let participants reason about
Authority, accountability, and scope; they do not represent actual law or industry policy.

## Initial value proposition

The sponsor proposes that C1 may:

- reduce time spent sorting standardized documents;
- improve consistency of draft memoranda;
- preserve underwriter capacity for individualized analysis;
- shorten application-to-decision time for eligible applications; and
- generate useful evidence about whether a bounded AI-assisted workflow is worth pursuing.

Participants must distinguish observed task outcomes from organizational or applicant Value. They
must also consider C0 and later alternatives rather than treating any positive outcome as sufficient.

## Initial independent risk proposition

The independent reviewer begins with plausible concerns about:

- unsupported or misleading draft statements;
- loss of source accessibility;
- different performance for nonstandard records;
- automation bias or over-reliance, especially among less-experienced users;
- inappropriate expansion beyond the bounded use;
- privacy, security, and vendor dependence;
- reduced individualization or unequal applicant effects; and
- insufficient capacity to verify drafts at the proposed throughput.

These are candidate Risk concerns, not predetermined conclusions. Participants may find some
unsupported, discover different risks, or judge that the proposed controls do not address the material
concern.

## Initial evidence packet

The exercise begins with:

- a vendor capability description and limitation statement;
- a small internal test on standardized synthetic application packages;
- a workflow time baseline for C0;
- a human-review procedure for C1;
- a proposed eligible-use definition;
- the fictional policy and authority artifacts;
- a vendor security summary with unresolved questions; and
- a non-AI checklist improvement already feasible under C0.

The packet is intentionally incomplete. It does not establish realized Value, broad population
performance, causal attribution, continuing control effectiveness, or safe expansion.

## Decision object

At each scenario point, the practitioner must state the exact proposed action. Available actions may
include declining the proposal, requesting more evidence, authorizing a bounded stage, continuing
unchanged, narrowing, changing Configuration, suspending, or ending the activity. The list is not a
ranking and PAIM must not choose among them.

Any authorization must bind the current Configuration, selected Value and Risk bases, explicit
Boundary, evidence and Authority available at that time. Later evidence does not rewrite the prior
Decision; it may change what action is legitimate now.

## Case boundaries

- Numerical observations are fictional exercise inputs, not benchmarks or findings about lending.
- No participant is asked to make an individual loan decision.
- The case avoids claims about actual legal admissibility; participants apply only the fictional policy
  and authority packet.
- The exercise does not require PAIM and RWR to agree.
- Value and Risk may use some of the same evidence but must retain independent interpretations.
- A successful run does not establish PAIM effectiveness.
